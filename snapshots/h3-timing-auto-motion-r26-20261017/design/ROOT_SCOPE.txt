R26 — Automatic local motion monitoring and intervention
Updated 2026-10-17 CST. Active research, no publication-level novelty established.

User requirement
Finish R24 and current prepared work rigorously. Collect denser action sequences,
screen ordinary edits, and investigate repairs on clearly erroneous examples.
The deployed candidate must take a source video, identity reference and editing
instruction, with no supplied action interval or denoising start/stop interval.
Within the same generation it must locate discrepancies, decide when evidence is
usable, start intervention, stop, and continue monitoring for recurrence.
After repeatable benefit, compare two close released training-free methods on
the same sources. Preserve normal cases and every screened generation.

Current evidence
R24 consists of two fresh sources and two seeds with paired Native/Full.
The four Native primary windows are near synchronized on usable measurements.
These cases support normal-control and false-intervention analysis. They do not
demonstrate correction of an initially substantial timing defect.
R19 demonstrated dynamic stopping with an oracle action interval and fixed
early intervention. Its actual trajectory equals fixed R18 on that case.
Automatic interval discovery, automatic first reliable observation, and useful
instance-dependent activation remain unverified.
R25 is an already-prepared two-arm reverse-reference-edge causal experiment on
the old clear failure. Finish its bounded experiment and use the result to decide
whether that edge warrants inclusion. Its fixed interval is diagnostic only.

Work sequence and evidence gates
1. Complete all R24 archives and four paired presentations, retaining UNKNOWN,
   out-of-window degradation, elapsed time and actual model-call counts.
2. Source-only selection: collect at least three new candidate recordings with
   repeated action changes. Record source provenance, visibility and selection
   criteria before examining generated outputs. Prepare equal geometry/rate.
   Source trim selection can be automatic. It is distinct from an intervention
   window, which the eventual method must derive during generation.
3. Run a predeclared Native screen. Keep the complete screen ledger, including
   normal, erroneous and unmeasurable results. Error-enriched development cases
   will not be represented as an unbiased benchmark or failure prevalence.
4. CPU observer feasibility using archived trajectories: scan the entire physical
   timeline and parts; retain all tested locations, confidence failures and
   detector ambiguity. Start with a transparent sliding-window baseline before
   adding a new mechanism. Existing hand-picked windows are retrospective
   references only and cannot enter the new detector's inputs.
5. Evaluate a source-derived calibration and observability mechanism. A temporal
   tolerance such as two physical frames defines the desired task accuracy;
   detector acceptance thresholds and intervention strength are separate. Any
   data-derived threshold must be fit without the held-out generated outcome.
   Correlated windows and checkpoints cannot be counted as independent samples.
6. Run a fully online prototype after frozen detector tests. Observe the current
   native endpoint on the actual edited trajectory. Logged decisions must give
   physical-frame support, body-part support, uncertainty, the eligible operator,
   denoising index/sigma, actual NFE and integrated update. Reading a saved final
   video, selecting a window and rerunning does not satisfy the online objective.
7. Freeze the promising rule and repeat across clear failures, new source/seed
   cases and normal controls. Compare timing, same-frame shape, edit fidelity,
   appearance, artifacts, false activation and cost. An internal sensor score
   alone is insufficient evidence of successful editing.
8. Audit and deploy two close open methods when the candidate passes the previous
   gate. Record repository commit, weights, operator transplant differences and
   available input conditions. A backbone adaptation receives a separate label
   from reproduction of the authors' full method.

Constraints for algorithm design
All-time monitoring begins conceptually from the first native endpoint. Early
unobservable states produce an explicit abstention. A fixed step at which a new
sensor is enabled remains a manual design parameter and must not be described
as an automatically inferred reliable stage.
Existing gap5 spans about 17 RGB frames and may alias repeated actions. It is a
coarse cue, not proof of two-frame alignment or disappearance of short actions.
Pose confidence, view changes and occlusion require rejection; a low detector
response is not an omitted action. Morphology changes can affect relative-wrist
features, so bone directions and independent visual checks remain necessary.
Source-only activity selection, source autocorrelation and empirical quantiles
may provide useful calibration, but none alone establishes a novel principle or
valid coverage probability. Test actual RGB/time perturbations, amplitude loss,
occlusion, repeated cycles and normal cross-identity edits separately.
Potential architecture changes must have a concrete operator and a minimal
ablation proving an additional effect. New acronyms and automatic wiring are
not evidence of a new mechanism.

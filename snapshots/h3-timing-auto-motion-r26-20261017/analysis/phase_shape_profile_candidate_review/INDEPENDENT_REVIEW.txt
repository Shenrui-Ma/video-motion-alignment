Mathematical/implementation reading
For each bone, c=sum(w*x*conj(y_d)) and the minimized weighted SO(2) squared error is 2*(W-|c|); summing these terms and dividing by total W is implemented correctly. One constant rotation per bone and window can absorb static bone-angle offsets, including changes that are real pose/edit errors. Hence low profile error alone is not preserved-action evidence; raw zero-lag error and fitted rotations must accompany it.
The mask and minimum weights are fixed across all 49 candidate lags within each confidence threshold. Lag selection then minimizes the maximum loss of the two thresholds; all gates require their joint support. This removes a candidate-dependent support advantage but is deliberately restrictive: even perfect full-frame visibility supplies shifted data only at physical frames 24..116. The requirement that every frame in a proposed interval has some valid bone prevents intervals spanning that unsupported edge from qualifying. Missing values are not interpolated. Shape/time-schema errors remain technical errors, whereas finite-data support failures remain UNKNOWN.
The angular-increment variance J subtracts a weighted mean separately per bone, then aggregates edge energy over bones. Its two-frame scale is a local information heuristic after profiling the constant angle offset, not a global uniqueness theorem. Uniform angular velocity is confounded with constant bone rotation; the K=0 result is appropriate. The near-optimal lag-set, width, null-intersection and boundary gates add needed checks for periodic/multiple-phase ambiguity. Interval edge sums exclude the preceding edge correctly. The non-overlapping DP, positive value and deterministic tie rules match the frozen definition; physical-window segmentation has no denoising-step start/stop logic.
The 0.05-radian tolerance, concentration cut, J gate and complexity-cost expression remain fixed engineering choices. Naming the last expression a code length does not provide a statistical error guarantee or an empirically calibrated MDL penalty. Common-mask observations and overlapping intervals are dependent.

What the synthetic record establishes
Constant per-bone offsets are removed in the specified nuisance model: the central null RMS is about 3.03e-8 while raw errors remain nonzero. The eight high-information true-shift cases recover the imposed lags with/without those offsets. Null shifts, uniform angular velocity, periodic ambiguity and invalid/missing data follow the registered outcomes.
The eight old zero-warp stress cases all return K=0, but their supported intervals are rejected first by low J. This does not isolate nuisance profiling as the reason their previous false positives disappear. The four old low-curvature true-shift cases also return K=0, showing the real coverage tradeoff rather than successful discrimination on that family.
All selected positive full-support intervals are the same central 93-frame span [24,116]. The current positive evidence therefore establishes identifiable global constant-shift recovery on this family; it does not establish short local-event localization or multiple disjoint phase segments. The missing-data cases establish data handling, not that stronger claim. These limitations need not block the cheaper, unchanged 63-endpoint descriptive evaluation.

Required real-evaluation boundary
1. Freeze a new adapter/input manifest, retaining the synthetic v1 unchanged. Specify the exact COCO bone directions, coordinate normalization, valid-length/finite rules, confidence reduction, frame grid and every input SHA. profile.contexts currently uses minimum raw confidence without clipping. Historical DWPose scores can exceed 1; the adapter must explicitly state that weight convention rather than silently inherit another metric's min(...).clip(0,1). The numerical kernel/tolerances/cost/DP must remain unchanged for this evaluation.
2. Score all 63 declared endpoints, including normal, early, recovery and difficult samples. Preserve technical failures separately from UNKNOWN, every rejection reason and raw/profile error. A K=0 output can mean lack of evidence, lack of support or a nonpositive objective; it is not a certificate of alignment. Report eligible temporal coverage, both arms, selected intervals and full rejection counts. Do not select only endpoints/windows that this observer finds easy.
3. The old endpoint labels and pose readouts are development references, not independent semantic truth. Report disagreement and UNKNOWN. Do not retune after reading the 63 outcomes or convert overlapping intervals into independent sample counts.
4. Do not connect this observer to the live controller based on this pass. A later claim of local phase segmentation requires local/piecewise-shift positives and shape/phase-confounding controls; an online claim additionally requires a causal intervention comparison and normal preservation. Those are later evidence needs, not extra work imposed on the present descriptive batch.

Decision rule for the permitted next step
Useful evidence is retained nonzero phase detection on appropriate known delayed endpoints while normal/near-aligned cases remain compatible with null, with sufficient independently interpretable coverage. If nearly all cases become UNKNOWN, or nuisance fitting removes the action differences of interest, record that limitation and stop this version; do not relax support/J/tolerances to recover a desired outcome. No probability, conformal coverage, universal accuracy, automatic-start/stop or novelty claim follows from this review.

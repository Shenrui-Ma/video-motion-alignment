A separate narrow actuator possibility is the odd part of reference-reading temporal displacement, rather than selecting one displaced reference read. Current H3PhaseRoPE/core.py uses O_new=O_native+alpha*m_ref*(R(q_rot(delta))-R(q)). At a single block, the proposed direction is T_delta=m_ref*(R(q_rot(delta))-R(q_rot(-delta)))/(2*delta), and O_new=O_native+eta*T_delta. This is a finite-difference derivative with respect to reference-reading phase. The constant/even component cancels algebraically at the frozen Q/K/V block; nonlinear later blocks and appearance information in temporal derivatives remain. It does not guarantee identity preservation, monotone phase control or final quality.

As delta→0, dR/dtau=E_P[(V-R)*(dq/dtau dot K/sqrt(d))]. This is the softmax derivative/covariance identity, not itself a new mechanism. Preserve original reference mass and all untouched output rows, but signed derivative residual need not correspond to positive attention probabilities. A gain determined from an observed delay would require an actually measured direction-to-output-phase response; its sign is not known a priori.
